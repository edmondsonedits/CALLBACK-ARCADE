# Sketch & Sorcery — three-round exhibition

For 3–10 players. New matches use persisted showdown version 3. Already-saved version-2 matches retain two rounds, unmultiplied scores and their original lifecycle; legacy relay matches retain their separate rules.

1. Each player submits one short drawing prompt, original or borrowed from the 100-idea catalogue. Anonymous ballots rank up to three prompts for 3/2/1 election points. No self-voting. Identical texts merge, examples fill shortages, player-written prompts win tied votes, and remaining ties use the persisted private shuffle. The top three distinct prompts become the three rounds.
2. Round 1: everyone draws the first elected prompt for four minutes (six with extended timers). Submitting locks the sketch; all players finishing advances early. Deadline submission attempts preserve unfinished marked drawings.
3. Round 2: the lowest scorer after round 1 chooses one of three offered drawing curses for everyone. A stable private shuffle resolves tied last place and selects three distinct offers from `circles`, `giant-brush`, `no-erase`, `limited-palette`, `no-fill`, `lines`. The chooser and offers are stored before selection. The chooser has 15 seconds (22.5 extended); a bot or timeout chooses the first stored offer. No host bypass. All round-2 laughter and correct-guess awards are doubled.
4. Round 3: the curse lifts. Before drawing, each player chooses one guided modifier (at a royal banquet, inside a tiny teacup, or saving the kingdom) for the third elected prompt, plus a starter sketch: sword, shield, crown, person or dragon. Preparation lasts 30 seconds (45 extended), or until everyone locks a choice. Bots and missing choices use deterministic server defaults. A submitted choice cannot change. The full individual assignment is shown only on that player's phone until their artist reveal. All final-round laughter and correct-guess awards are tripled.
5. Each round uses the existing allowance-protected reference-image transformation. The sketch remains the sole source of the AI subject. The individual **drawing assignment** is retained in the immutable image-job metadata and reveal, but is not appended to the provider instruction. The exact **AI transformation prompt** is shared from `lib/sketch-transform-prompt.ts` and expandable on phone reveal/archive entries. These are labelled separately. Bots use free SVG practice drawings and never enqueue paid work. Anonymous bot artwork may include a restriction/starter rendering descriptor, without authorship or the private prompt. A bounded processing window ends in an all-original gallery if any real transformation is missing.
6. A shuffled anonymous tour gives every submitted drawing ten seconds and a stable letter. Artwork stays contained without cropping. Phones then rank up to three other drawings. Base laughter awards are 100/75/50 per ballot; round 2 awards 200/150/100; round 3 awards 300/225/150.
7. Players guess the artists behind the drawings they ranked. Each correct guess awards the voter 50 in round 1, 100 in round 2, or 150 in round 3. Each name may appear once per ballot. Every guess locks before any artist or individual assignment is disclosed.
8. Reveal drawings from lowest to highest laughter points, treating ties equally. Each artist gets twenty seconds. Show the original beside the transformation, the actual drawing assignment, artist name and saved avatar. Only the current artist's authenticated phone controls dance, cheer, wave, bow, shrug and facepalm. Reveal and replay release uniquely keyed score events once. Multipliers apply once to individual events, never again to cumulative totals.
9. Scoreboards separate the rounds. After the third round, tied leaders share the crown. Phones retain all revealed entries and their individual drawing assignments. A past round-2 drawing keeps its historical rendering restriction in the archive; that restriction does not apply to round 3.

Missed ballots and guesses abstain. Missing drawings are excluded. Zero-submission matches still complete. Originals remain private until authorized revelation or the whole-gallery fallback. Phase, deadlines, chooser eligibility, assignments, defaults, scoring, guesses and avatar ownership are server-authoritative. PNG uploads cannot prove which editor tools produced their pixels; the server supplies the locked restriction and the controller/editor enforces available tools.

The scripted browser narrator follows the persisted round count and multiplier. Its 2,000 original lines cover 25 situations in eight comic moods, with ten alternatives per situation and mood. Automatic mood stays consistent for the match; the TV can select a different mood. Shuffled line decks and heard-event history avoid repeated polling speech. Final preparation/drawing instructions direct artists to their private phones without reading private assignments. Voice requires a host gesture; captions, mute/pause and reduced-motion controls remain available. Research, the full scriptbook and runtime contracts: docs/sketch-narrator-research.md.
